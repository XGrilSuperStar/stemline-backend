Hum to notes

Hum, sing or whistle a tune and see the notes

  1. Log in on the main page first.
  2. Record. Press Record, then hum, sing or whistle one note at a time. Stay close to the mic and keep it under a minute.
  3. Or upload a recording of your voice instead.
  4. Notes appear on their own. When you stop recording or pick a file, it listens and writes out the melody with tempo and key.
  5. Download (Premium). Save the melody as MIDI or MusicXML to drop into FL Studio or any notation software.
  • Works best on one voice, one note at a time, in a quiet room
  • Free: on-screen preview
  • Premium: full chart + MIDI & MusicXML downloads

What Hum to Notes does

Hum to Notes listens to a short recording of your voice and writes down the melody you sang. It is a quick way to capture a tune that is stuck in your head, sketch a hook before you forget it, or turn a vocal idea into a MIDI part for your music program. Hum, sing, or whistle, and you get sheet music with the tempo and key, plus a MIDI file you can open in a DAW such as FL Studio.

Tips for better results

  • Record in a quiet room with no music playing behind you.
  • Sing or hum one clear note at a time. Slide between notes as little as you can.
  • Use "doo" or "la" instead of a long hum, so each note has a clear start.
  • Keep a steady pace. A short pause before you start helps.
  • Stay under a minute. Short phrases give the cleanest result.

Hum to Notes questions

Does it work with more than one voice?

No. It follows a single melody line. Chords, harmonies and backing music will confuse it.

Why are some notes wrong?

Notes are detected automatically from the sound of your voice, so a wobbly pitch or a noisy room can cause mistakes. Treat the result as a first draft and fix anything that sounds off.

Can I use this for free?

Yes. Free accounts see the first few measures on screen. Premium unlocks the full chart and the MIDI and MusicXML downloads.

What can I open the downloads in?

MIDI opens in music production programs. MusicXML opens in notation software such as MuseScore, Finale, Sibelius and Dorico.

Want to write out the parts of a full song instead? Split a song on the main page and use Sheet music.
